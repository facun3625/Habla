Effects of the PROMPT intervention in a girl with childhood apraxia of speech: a case study

María Virginia Trotta

Speech-Language Pathologist · License No. 263/1F8 L1
Santa Fe, Argentina | mavirginiatrotta@gmail.com

1. Introduction

Childhood apraxia of speech (CAS) is a motor speech disorder characterized by difficulties in planning and programming the movements needed to produce precise, consistent and intelligible speech (ASHA, 2007).

PROMPT (Prompts for Restructuring Oral Muscular Phonetic Targets) is a tactile-kinesthetic approach that organizes intervention according to the Motor Speech Hierarchy and integrates the physical-sensory, cognitive-linguistic and social-emotional domains (Hayden, 2006; PROMPT Institute, 2019).

2. Objective

To describe the motor, articulatory and communicative changes observed after a PROMPT-based intervention in a 6-year-old girl with childhood apraxia of speech co-occurring with a predominantly expressive developmental language disorder.

3. Case presentation

A 6-year-old girl with a clinical diagnosis of childhood apraxia of speech and a predominantly expressive developmental language disorder.

Initial characteristics

  • Low speech intelligibility.
  • Inconsistent consonant productions.
  • Altered coarticulatory timing.
  • Prosodic and speech rhythm impairments.
  • Difficulty maintaining the jaw at midline.
  • Compensatory lip hyperretraction.
  • Reduced lip rounding and protrusion.
  • Difficulty with tongue-jaw dissociation.
  • Jaw instability and lateralization.
  • Predominant use of single words and short phrases.

4. Communication focus areas

1 · Speech subsystem control

Improve intelligibility by developing more stable, symmetrical and varied jaw and labial-facial movements, and promote anterior and mid-posterior tongue contraction in words and short phrases.

2 · Interactive communication routines

Promote verbal interaction through turn-taking, initiating exchanges, asking questions and producing answers.

5. Methodology

Design

Single-case, longitudinal, descriptive clinical study.

Intervention

  • Period: August to November 2025.
  • Frequency: 2 sessions per week.
  • Session length: 45 minutes.
  • Total duration: 4 months.
  • Approximate total: 32 sessions.

6. Assessment and diagnosis

The diagnosis of childhood apraxia of speech was clinical, based on the developmental history, the motor speech analysis and the presence of features consistent with CAS.

The complementary instruments were used to characterize the overall clinical profile and thus carry out the differential diagnosis.

Complementary instruments

  • VMPAC: nonverbal oral movements, to explore oral praxis difficulties.
  • CELF-5: language profile and documentation of the co-occurring language disorder.
  • MABC-2: administered by Occupational Therapy, as background on the global motor profile.
  • FOCUS: pre-post functional measure completed by the family.

8. Performance recording and analysis

Performance recording and analysis

Each session included a structured motor warm-up with 19 target words, each practiced 3 times (≈57 productions).

Then, during games, routines and functional activities, the same 19 words were used in a distributed manner, reaching approximately 100 total productions per session (≈5 productions per word on average).

The reported percentages are clinical estimates based on these production opportunities, considering the accuracy of the motor pattern and the level of assistance required.

10. PROMPT intervention

Jaw, labial-facial and lingual control were prioritized through parameter, syllable and surface PROMPTs.

Therapeutic progression

  1. Jaw stabilization
  2. Labial-facial control
  3. Tongue-jaw dissociation
  4. Integration into functional words and phrases

Prioritized phonemes and movements

  • Bilabial contact: /p/, /b/, /m/.
  • Rounding and protrusion: /o/, /u/.
  • Anterior linguals: /t/, /n/, /d/.
  • Mid-posterior tongue contraction.
  • Jaw stability and midline control.
  • Jaw – lip – tongue coordination.

Activities

  • Symbolic play and turn-taking games.
  • Personalized stories.
  • Functional phrases and communication routines.
  • Home practice with caregivers.
  • Coordination with school and other professionals.

11. External clinical review

  • Video recordings, reassessment and progress analysis reviewed by a certified PROMPT instructor.
  • Highest score awarded by the instructor for demonstration of change between sessions and for reassessment and progress analysis.
  • No formal inter-rater agreement index was calculated.

12. Ethical considerations

  • Institutional consent form of the PROMPT Institute signed by the legal guardians, authorizing audiovisual recording for research and educational purposes.
  • Project and recordings reviewed within the formal PROMPT certification process.
  • Not reviewed by an independent ethics committee.

14. Functional results

  • Greater intelligibility
  • Greater verbal initiative
  • Greater communicative participation
  • 2- and 3-word phrases
  • Better tolerance of tactile contact
  • Fewer compensatory movements

In the qualitative analysis

  • Reduced lip hyperretraction.
  • Greater articulatory symmetry and jaw stability.
  • Greater lip-jaw independence.

In communication

  • More spontaneous use of words and 2–3-word phrases.
  • Greater communicative intent and participation.
  • Greater verbal autonomy and desire to communicate.

The physical-sensory domain remained at 60%: it includes structural, sensory and gross motor variables that remained affected, so this stability does not imply an absence of motor changes.

The specific analysis of the speech motor system did show improvements in jaw stability, bilabial contact, lip protrusion and articulatory coordination.

The cognitive-linguistic and social-emotional gains reflect greater spontaneous vocabulary, initiative, turn-taking and self-regulation.

16. FOCUS — pre / post comparison

FOCUS (Focus on the Outcomes of Communication Under Six) is a functional measure completed by parents/caregivers that assesses how the child communicates in real, everyday situations. It evaluates functional communication, including body functions, activity/capacity and social participation.

PrePost
2 or less: significant difficulty 4: basic functional performance 6 or more: near-typical functioning
Overall post-intervention score: 19/25 (76%)

17. Conclusions

  • The PROMPT intervention was associated with clinically relevant changes in jaw stability, labial-facial and lingual coordination, accuracy of articulatory movements, and functional communication.
  • The progressive reduction in the support needed and the emergence of more stable motor patterns suggest progress toward motor speech autonomy.
  • FOCUS and the progression of the cognitive-linguistic and social-emotional domains complemented the motor findings, with greater participation and communicative functionality.
  • The unchanged physical-sensory score does not imply an absence of specific motor changes, but rather the persistence of structural, sensory and global motor variables not modified over four months.
  • As this is a single-case study, the findings do not allow causal relationships to be established and cannot be generalized.

18. Future directions

Continue longitudinal recording until clinical discharge to characterize the timing, sequence and types of change observed.

In future studies

  • Systematize the recording of the level of assistance.
  • Include independent raters.
  • Use specific intelligibility measures in functional contexts.
  • Expand the number of cases and develop comparative designs.
  • Replicate with a control group when methodologically feasible.
